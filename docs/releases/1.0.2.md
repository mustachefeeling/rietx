# rietx 1.0.2

A documentation release on the 1.0.x road, which is where the public call
surface is frozen chapter by chapter. No source file changed, so 1.0.2 computes
what 1.0.1 computed and every number in
[VALIDATION.md](https://github.com/yue-here/rietx/blob/main/docs/VALIDATION.md)
still stands as measured.

## What changed

- **New chapter: "Patterns, structures and instruments".** The reference for
  the three objects a refinement takes, field by field: `PatternData` and where
  σ comes from, `Structure` with `Phase`, `Cell` and `Atom`, the optional
  displacement, strain and orientation blocks, and `Instrument` with its three
  presets, source, geometry, width function and background models. It also
  states the calibrate → freeze → refine-sample workflow in terms of what each
  plan frees.
- **New chapter: "The parameter table".** The flat table between those objects
  and the solver: the dot-path grammar and its one trap (no brackets, because
  globs match with `fnmatch`), `Refinement.parameters` and the `ParameterRow` it
  returns, the three separate reasons a row is held and why `refinable` is the
  one predicate to read, how physical bounds map into the transformed variable
  the optimiser actually varies, and the two editing verbs with the four
  refusals each can raise. It closes on the fit's own view of the same table,
  `RefinementResult.parameters`, whose membership rule is a contract: a row
  appears if the entry varied or was tied, so a fixed parameter is absent rather
  than present with `vary=False`.
- **New chapter: "Running a refinement".** Everything around the staged fit
  that "How a refinement works" argues for: the two entry points and why
  `backend` and `solver` belong to a `Refinement` while `mode` and `plan`
  belong to one `fit`; the three intensity modes; the plan registry
  (`PLAN_INFO`, `PLAN_PRESETS`, `PlanInfo`) a program reads instead of
  hard-coding a preset list; the four solver settings a `Stage` carries, with
  `seed` and `strain_seed` distinguished by their opposite pathologies at zero;
  `PlanSpec` and `StageSpec`, and why `preset_name` is derived by comparison
  rather than stored; `run_stage`; `StageResult`; `GuardFinding` and its six
  constructors; `events=`; `cancel=` with `RefinementCancelled`; and
  `Provenance`.
- **New chapter: "The refinement history".** `Refinement.history` as an object
  rather than as a file: what a `HistoryNode` holds and why it stores state and
  not curves, the `NodeAction` that produced it (and why a stage records its own
  solver settings, so `Refinement.cherry_pick` replays the stage that ran), the
  `RefinementState` a checkout puts back, the as-optimised `NodeMetrics` and how
  they differ from a `replay`, the tree's queries, and the four verbs that go
  back or fork: `Refinement.checkout`, `Refinement.branch`,
  `Refinement.cherry_pick` and `Refinement.merge`.
- **New chapter: "Indexing an unknown cell".** The three calls that find a
  lattice when the specimen is unidentified, and the objects they return:
  `pick_peaks` with `PeakList`, `ObservedPeak` and the twelve line flags (five
  of which take a line out of `PeakList.usable`); `DataQualityReport`, where
  "can this be searched" is a per-system question and a separate one from "can
  it be scored"; the systematic-shift screen, where `ShiftScreen.allowance_deg`
  is what a search window must span and `ShiftScreen.sigma_sys_deg` is not, and
  the two differ by 4.3× on a certified pattern; `index_pattern` argument by
  argument, with the presets, the two budgets and the three states a crystal
  system can end a run in; `IndexingResult` and `CellCandidate` with the
  figure-of-merit panel, the Bravais opinions, the ambiguity partners and the
  whole-profile Le Bail test; the twelve confidence caveats, split into the six
  that refute a cell and the six that only cap its grade; the evidence view for
  a consumer that can reason; and `determine_extinction_symbol`, which answers
  with an extinction *class* and lists the space groups it cannot separate.
- **New chapter: "Refining many patterns".** The two ways to have more than one
  pattern, and why they are not variants of each other: a series is N separate
  refinements chained by a warm start, a joint fit is one residual sharing the
  parameters that describe the specimen, and the question that picks between
  them is whether the specimen changed. `SequentialRefinement` and
  `refine_sequential` with `carry`, the `prepare` hook and the per-pattern
  history; `SeriesResult`, `SeriesEntry` and `Trajectory`, including why a
  trajectory skips a missing point rather than filling it; the escalation ladder
  and the quarantine, with `SeriesEntry.rung` and `SeriesEntry.reseeded`
  answering different questions; the four `SEQUENTIAL_*` fences and what
  `direction="both"` is for. Then `MultiHistogramRefinement`, `refine_multi`,
  `SharingMap` and its serializable twin `SharingSpec`, and `HistogramResult` —
  where a pooled Rwp is never quoted alone.
- **New chapter: "How much of each phase".** Quantitative phase analysis, which
  had no chapter at all: `QuantitativePhaseAnalysis` and the `PhaseQuantity`
  row, with the Z·M·V quantities separated from the best-effort integer split
  that no weight fraction depends on; the crystalline-only scope stated as the
  two things it hides — an amorphous fraction and a missing phase, both of which
  leave a set summing to 1; and microabsorption, where the corrected fraction is
  reported beside the uncorrected one rather than substituted for it, and
  `PhaseQuantity.mu_r` travels with the answer so `BRINDLEY_OUTSIDE_REGIME` can
  point at it.
- **New chapter: "Exports and derived tables".** What a finished fit will
  compute on request: the calculated pattern, `RefinementResult.sig` as the one
  authority every weighted residual in the package divides by,
  `RefinementResult.ticks` covering every emission line rather than the primary
  one, `Refinement.predict` on an arbitrary grid, the reflection list as one row
  per (emission line, reflection) with `ReflectionRow` field by field, and
  `format_su` with the three cases a format string gets wrong.
- **"Calling rietx from a program" gains the two types it moves.** The chapter
  described the call and neither its request nor its response. It now documents
  the discriminated request union branch by branch — `RefineRequest`,
  `MultiRefineRequest`, `SequentialRefineRequest`, `IndexRequest` and
  `SuggestRequest`, each key with its type and its default — and the response
  envelope: `AgentSuccess` with the four answer arms and the two companions that
  ride beside them, `AgentFailure`, `AgentError` and `AgentErrorDetail`. Three
  facts a JSON consumer needs and could not read anywhere: every arm is
  serialized including the null ones, so branch on which is non-null and not on
  which key exists; an empty `AgentErrorDetail.where` is a whole-request error
  rather than a field one; and `refine_json` strips the branch tag from every
  detail path, so `where` is a path into the dict as you wrote it. The
  `capabilities()` section also completes the arms it had named only in part:
  `BackendCapability`, `AnodeCapability`, `ReaderCapability`,
  `ReaderOptionCapability` and the three indexing vocabularies.
- **A resolved packaging note that still told you to work around it.** That
  chapter's tool-registration admonition said the agent protocol was reachable
  only by repository path and asked an integrator to paste it in by hand "until
  that is resolved at release". It was resolved at the release: the tool
  description points at the hosted copy *and* at an offline one inside the
  wheel, `rietx/data/AGENT_PROTOCOL.md`. Only the manual was out of date.
- **A missing backend does not report itself as one.** Measured on a build
  without the `jax` extra, `{"task": "refine", …, "backend": "jax"}` answers
  `REFINEMENT_FAILED` rather than `BACKEND_UNAVAILABLE`: the envelope maps
  `NotImplementedError` to that code and the backend registry raises
  `ImportError`. Nothing changed here — the vocabulary is documented as declared
  and the chapter carries the measurement as a warning, with
  `BackendCapability.available` as the question to ask before the call. The
  repair is WP-1076's.
- **"Files and projects" gains the project document.** The chapter already held
  the `.rex` directory; it now also documents the session object's six
  attributes, `ProjectDoc` field by field, and the four `DataRef` fields that
  describe the pattern. No separate projects chapter was written, because the
  directory, the one-authority rule and the data reference already lived here.
- **A corrected reflection-table row.** "Files and projects" described
  `write_reflection_table` as writing one row per reflection. It writes one row
  per **(emission line, reflection)**, so a Kα doublet gives two rows for the
  same hkl. Only the manual was wrong.
- **A corrected width table.** "How a refinement works" listed the Lorentzian
  profile terms as `x` for tan θ and `y` for 1/cos θ. The forward model is the
  other way round: `instrument.profile.x` is the 1/cos θ size term and
  `instrument.profile.y` the tan θ strain term. Only the manual was wrong, so no
  fit changes, but a plan written from that table freed the wrong term.

## The freeze

**170 names moved from provisional to frozen**, which is what a new Part 1
chapter does: [compatibility](https://yue-here.github.io/rietx/using/compatibility.html)
promises that a name Part 1 documents is frozen from the release that documents
it. The whole of `Parameter`, `PatternData`, `Cell`, `Atom`, `AnisoU`,
`PreferredOrientation`, `StephensStrain`, `Source`, `EmissionLine`,
`Dispersion`, `ProfileTCHZ`, the three background models and the two surface
roughness models is now frozen, along with the remaining fields of `Structure`,
`Phase`, `Instrument` and `Geometry`.

The parameter-table chapter freezes 29 more: the whole of `ParameterRow`,
`RefinedParameter` apart from `initial` and `at_bound`, `TieSpec` apart from
`TieSpec.from_tie`, `Refinement.parameters`, `Refinement.set_vary`,
`Refinement.set_values`, `Refinement.structure`, `Refinement.instrument` and
`RefinementResult.parameters`.

The refining chapter freezes 71 more: the whole of `Stage`, `StageResult`,
`StageSpec`, `PlanSpec`, `PlanInfo`, `GuardFinding`, `Provenance` and
`RefinementCancelled`, plus `PLAN_INFO`, `PLAN_PRESETS`,
`RefinementPlan.correlation_guard`, `Refinement.run_stage`,
`Refinement.snapshot`, `Refinement.result_`, `Refinement.stage_reports_` and
`RefinementResult.stages`, `.provenance` and `.mode`.

The history and project chapters freeze 117 more, and eleven types are now
frozen in full: `RefinementTree`, `HistoryNode`, `NodeAction`, `NodeMetrics`,
`RefinementState`, `ReflectionState`, `TreeHeader`, `Annotation`, `Project`,
`ProjectDoc` and `DataRef`. With them go `replay`, `Refinement.branch`,
`Refinement.merge`, `Refinement.cherry_pick`, `Refinement.from_node`,
`RefinementResult.node_id` and `RefinementResult.tree_id`.

The indexing chapter freezes 266 more, and every indexing type is now frozen in
full: `PeakList`, `ObservedPeak`, `DataQualityReport`, `ShiftScreen`,
`ShiftTemplateFit`, `ReflectionPairScreen`, `SearchSpecSpec`,
`IndexingControls`, `IndexingResult`, `CellCandidate`, `FigureOfMerit`,
`BravaisOpinion`, `AmbiguityPartner`, `LeBailValidation`, `IndexingEvidence`,
`CandidateEvidence`, `CaveatEvidence`, `ExtinctionScreen` and
`ExtinctionCandidate`, together with `pick_peaks`, `index_pattern`,
`determine_extinction_symbol`, `EngineCapability` and the rest of
`SearchPresetCapability`. Nothing indexing-related is left in the provisional
bucket.

The series chapter freezes 83 more, and eight types with it: `SeriesResult`,
`SeriesEntry`, `Trajectory`, `SequentialRefinement`, `MultiHistogramRefinement`,
`HistogramResult`, `SharingMap` and `SharingSpec`, together with
`RefinementResult.histograms` and `RefinementResult.for_histogram`.

The phase-analysis and exports chapters freeze 49 more, and four types:
`QuantitativePhaseAnalysis`, `PhaseQuantity`, `MicroabsorptionCorrection` and
`ReflectionRow`. With them go `RefinementResult.qpa`, `RefinementResult.sig`,
`RefinementResult.sigma`, `RefinementResult.ticks`, `Refinement.predict`,
`Refinement.reflection_table`, the three `Refinement.write_*` methods,
`reflection_table` and `format_su`.

The agent chapter freezes 101 more, and thirteen types with it: the five request
types, `AgentSuccess`, `AgentFailure`, `AgentError`, `AgentErrorDetail`,
`BackendCapability`, `AnodeCapability`, `ReaderCapability` and
`ReaderOptionCapability` are now frozen in full, and so is `Capabilities`
itself. `SuggestionResult` is the hundred-and-first: the chapter names it as the
type of the `suggestion` arm, which freezes the name while its own fields stay
provisional for the report chapter.

The names left out are left out for one reason: a chapter freezes what it
documents, so a name it cannot describe honestly stays provisional rather than
earning a passing mention. `TieSpec.from_tie` converts a type that is not itself
public. `RefinedParameter.initial` and `RefinedParameter.at_bound` are written
by nothing in the package, and `at_bound` serializes as `false` on every row,
which reads as a measurement rather than as the absence of one.
`RefinementResult.correlation_warnings` is the same shape found one chapter
later: nothing in the package writes it or reads it, so it serializes as an
empty list, which reads as "no correlations were flagged" rather than as "this
field is not filled in". All of these are being reconsidered in WP-1076 rather
than frozen as they stand.

**The surface itself grew by 24 names, and none of them are new API.** A
dataclass field with no default is never assigned on the class, so the
derivation that enumerates the public surface could not see `Stage.name`,
`Stage.turn_on`, the four fields of `GuardFinding` and `PlanInfo`, eleven of
`ReflectionRow`, and six more — while counting the defaulted fields beside
them. They were public all along and simply absent from the count. The
enumeration now reads dataclass fields explicitly; the effect on a user is that
the denominator is honest, and 24 names that were never in either bucket are
now in one.

Build on the frozen names. The surface is 1322 names, of which 1160 are now
frozen and 162 remain provisional. The compatibility chapter's rule is unchanged
for anything still provisional: it works as it stands, and the release notes say
when it moves.

## Upgrading

Nothing to do. No behavioural difference and no contract version moved.
