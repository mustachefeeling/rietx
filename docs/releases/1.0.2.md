# rietx 1.0.2

> **This release was never published.** 1.0.2 was written, complete and
> unreleased when v1.1 opened, and its content shipped inside
> [1.1.0](1.1.0.md) instead — the fold was the maintainer's call on
> 2026-08-23, so that an upgrade from 1.0.1 crosses one release rather than
> two. There is no `1.0.2` on PyPI and no `v1.0.2` tag. This file stays as the
> chapter-by-chapter record of what that work was; **1.1.0's § Upgrading is
> the authority** on what a person has to act on.


Mostly a documentation release on the 1.0.x road, which is where the public
call surface is frozen chapter by chapter. **No forward model, solver or
statistic changed**, so every number in
[VALIDATION.md](https://github.com/yue-here/rietx/blob/main/docs/VALIDATION.md)
still stands as measured, and a refinement gives the answer 1.0.1 gave. Two
things did change. What a result *says*: two fields that nothing wrote have been
removed and one now answers a question instead of defaulting to an answer. And
what the **extinction screen** answers — writing the chapter over it found it
refuting a certified space group, and the repair changes that call's result on
data where a neighbouring peak's tail reaches a forbidden position. Both move
the schema contract, and § Upgrading says who has to do something about it.

One **promise** changed with them. Indexing is documented in full here and
deliberately not frozen: the subsystem is declared provisional, because it is
still under active development and the extinction repair above is what that
looks like from a caller's side.

## What changed

- **New chapter: "Patterns, structures and instruments".** The reference for
  the three objects a refinement takes, field by field: `PatternData` and where
  σ comes from, `Structure` with `Phase`, `Cell` and `Atom`, the optional
  displacement, strain and orientation blocks, and `Instrument` with its three
  presets, source, geometry, width function and background models. It also
  states the calibrate → freeze → refine-sample workflow in terms of what each
  plan frees.
- **New chapter: "The parameter table".** The flat table between those objects
  and the solver: the dot-path grammar and its one trap (no brackets, because
  globs match with `fnmatch`), `Refinement.parameters` and the `ParameterRow` it
  returns, the three separate reasons a row is held and why `refinable` is the
  one predicate to read, how physical bounds map into the transformed variable
  the optimiser actually varies, and the two editing verbs with the four
  refusals each can raise. It closes on the fit's own view of the same table,
  `RefinementResult.parameters`, whose membership rule is a contract: a row
  appears if the entry varied or was tied, so a fixed parameter is absent rather
  than present with `vary=False`.
- **New chapter: "Running a refinement".** Everything around the staged fit
  that "How a refinement works" argues for: the two entry points and why
  `backend` and `solver` belong to a `Refinement` while `mode` and `plan`
  belong to one `fit`; the three intensity modes; the plan registry
  (`PLAN_INFO`, `PLAN_PRESETS`, `PlanInfo`) a program reads instead of
  hard-coding a preset list; the four solver settings a `Stage` carries, with
  `seed` and `strain_seed` distinguished by their opposite pathologies at zero;
  `PlanSpec` and `StageSpec`, and why `preset_name` is derived by comparison
  rather than stored; `run_stage`; `StageResult`; `GuardFinding` and its six
  constructors; `events=`; `cancel=` with `RefinementCancelled`; and
  `Provenance`.
- **New chapter: "The refinement history".** `Refinement.history` as an object
  rather than as a file: what a `HistoryNode` holds and why it stores state and
  not curves, the `NodeAction` that produced it (and why a stage records its own
  solver settings, so `Refinement.cherry_pick` replays the stage that ran), the
  `RefinementState` a checkout puts back, the as-optimised `NodeMetrics` and how
  they differ from a `replay`, the tree's queries, and the four verbs that go
  back or fork: `Refinement.checkout`, `Refinement.branch`,
  `Refinement.cherry_pick` and `Refinement.merge`.
- **New chapter: "Indexing an unknown cell".** The three calls that find a
  lattice when the specimen is unidentified, and the objects they return:
  `pick_peaks` with `PeakList`, `ObservedPeak` and the twelve line flags (five
  of which take a line out of `PeakList.usable`); `DataQualityReport`, where
  "can this be searched" is a per-system question and a separate one from "can
  it be scored"; the systematic-shift screen, where `ShiftScreen.allowance_deg`
  is what a search window must span and `ShiftScreen.sigma_sys_deg` is not, and
  the two differ by 4.3× on a certified pattern; `index_pattern` argument by
  argument, with the presets, the two budgets and the three states a crystal
  system can end a run in; `IndexingResult` and `CellCandidate` with the
  figure-of-merit panel, the Bravais opinions, the ambiguity partners and the
  whole-profile Le Bail test; the twelve confidence caveats, split into the six
  that refute a cell and the six that only cap its grade; the evidence view for
  a consumer that can reason; and `determine_extinction_symbol`, which answers
  with an extinction *class* and lists the space groups it cannot separate.
- **New chapter: "Refining many patterns".** The two ways to have more than one
  pattern, and why they are not variants of each other: a series is N separate
  refinements chained by a warm start, a joint fit is one residual sharing the
  parameters that describe the specimen, and the question that picks between
  them is whether the specimen changed. `SequentialRefinement` and
  `refine_sequential` with `carry`, the `prepare` hook and the per-pattern
  history; `SeriesResult`, `SeriesEntry` and `Trajectory`, including why a
  trajectory skips a missing point rather than filling it; the escalation ladder
  and the quarantine, with `SeriesEntry.rung` and `SeriesEntry.reseeded`
  answering different questions; the four `SEQUENTIAL_*` fences and what
  `direction="both"` is for. Then `MultiHistogramRefinement`, `refine_multi`,
  `SharingMap` and its serializable twin `SharingSpec`, and `HistogramResult` —
  where a pooled Rwp is never quoted alone.
- **New chapter: "How much of each phase".** Quantitative phase analysis, which
  had no chapter at all: `QuantitativePhaseAnalysis` and the `PhaseQuantity`
  row, with the Z·M·V quantities separated from the best-effort integer split
  that no weight fraction depends on; the crystalline-only scope stated as the
  two things it hides — an amorphous fraction and a missing phase, both of which
  leave a set summing to 1; and microabsorption, where the corrected fraction is
  reported beside the uncorrected one rather than substituted for it, and
  `PhaseQuantity.mu_r` travels with the answer so `BRINDLEY_OUTSIDE_REGIME` can
  point at it.
- **New chapter: "Exports and derived tables".** What a finished fit will
  compute on request: the calculated pattern, `RefinementResult.sig` as the one
  authority every weighted residual in the package divides by,
  `RefinementResult.ticks` covering every emission line rather than the primary
  one, `Refinement.predict` on an arbitrary grid, the reflection list as one row
  per (emission line, reflection) with `ReflectionRow` field by field, and
  `format_su` with the three cases a format string gets wrong.
- **"Calling rietx from a program" gains the two types it moves.** The chapter
  described the call and neither its request nor its response. It now documents
  the discriminated request union branch by branch — `RefineRequest`,
  `MultiRefineRequest`, `SequentialRefineRequest`, `IndexRequest` and
  `SuggestRequest`, each key with its type and its default — and the response
  envelope: `AgentSuccess` with the four answer arms and the two companions that
  ride beside them, `AgentFailure`, `AgentError` and `AgentErrorDetail`. Three
  facts a JSON consumer needs and could not read anywhere: every arm is
  serialized including the null ones, so branch on which is non-null and not on
  which key exists; an empty `AgentErrorDetail.where` is a whole-request error
  rather than a field one; and `refine_json` strips the branch tag from every
  detail path, so `where` is a path into the dict as you wrote it. The
  `capabilities()` section also completes the arms it had named only in part:
  `BackendCapability`, `AnodeCapability`, `ReaderCapability`,
  `ReaderOptionCapability` and the three indexing vocabularies.
- **A resolved packaging note that still told you to work around it.** That
  chapter's tool-registration admonition said the agent protocol was reachable
  only by repository path and asked an integrator to paste it in by hand "until
  that is resolved at release". It was resolved at the release: the tool
  description points at the hosted copy *and* at an offline one inside the
  wheel, `rietx/data/AGENT_PROTOCOL.md`. Only the manual was out of date.
- **A missing backend does not report itself as one.** Measured on a build
  without the `jax` extra, `{"task": "refine", …, "backend": "jax"}` answers
  `REFINEMENT_FAILED` rather than `BACKEND_UNAVAILABLE`: the envelope maps
  `NotImplementedError` to that code and the backend registry raises
  `ImportError`. Nothing changed here — the vocabulary is documented as declared
  and the chapter carries the measurement as a warning, with
  `BackendCapability.available` as the question to ask before the call. The
  repair is WP-1076's.
- **"Files and projects" gains the project document.** The chapter already held
  the `.rex` directory; it now also documents the session object's six
  attributes, `ProjectDoc` field by field, and the four `DataRef` fields that
  describe the pattern. No separate projects chapter was written, because the
  directory, the one-authority rule and the data reference already lived here.
- **A corrected reflection-table row.** "Files and projects" described
  `write_reflection_table` as writing one row per reflection. It writes one row
  per **(emission line, reflection)**, so a Kα doublet gives two rows for the
  same hkl. Only the manual was wrong.
- **A corrected width table.** "How a refinement works" listed the Lorentzian
  profile terms as `x` for tan θ and `y` for 1/cos θ. The forward model is the
  other way round: `instrument.profile.x` is the 1/cos θ size term and
  `instrument.profile.y` the tan θ strain term. Only the manual was wrong, so no
  fit changes, but a plan written from that table freed the wrong term.
- **"The fit report" gains every type it moves.** The chapter described the
  three layers and named a fraction of the objects they carry. It now documents
  each one field by field: `Region` and `UnmatchedPeak` in Layer 0;
  `RegionAttribution` and `BasisCoefficient` in Layer 1, with the four gates now
  carrying the `GateFailure.code` a consumer branches on; `TrendAnalysis` and
  `TrendTemplate`; `TextureAnalysis` and `StrainAnalysis`, which had one
  sentence between them and now have a section, including why
  `StrainAnalysis.detected` measures the specimen rather than the residual and
  therefore does not go False when you model it; `BackgroundEvidence`,
  `IdentifiabilityEvidence`, `ExchangeFinding`, `CorrelationPair`, `SoftMode`
  and `LeBailGap` in the evidence sections; `SuggestedAction` in Layer 2; and
  `StageReport` in the trajectory. It also states that an abstention is **not
  monotone in Rwp** — the maturity gate is share-based, so a fit can improve and
  start abstaining, measured across a five-stage Le Bail plan whose Rwp falls
  from 3.17 to 0.146 while Layer 1 goes immature, immature, speaking,
  unreadable, unreadable.
- **The suggestion surface is documented, beside the layer it agrees with.**
  `Refinement.suggest` was named in one sentence and the object it returns
  nowhere. `SuggestionResult`, `CandidateGroup` and `ParameterCandidate` now sit
  next to Layer 2, because the two answer "what next" by independent methods —
  the residual's shape against the Jacobian's leverage — and
  `ParameterCandidate.action_kind` is the cross-reference that records when they
  name the same move.
- **The report's identifiability block was documented as the wrong type.**
  `FitReport.identifiability` is an `IdentifiabilityEvidence`; the chapter
  called it an `Identifiability`, which is the *result*'s block. The two share
  `top_correlations` and `soft_modes`, so every dotted name resolved and the
  coverage guard stayed green while three names were frozen against the wrong
  type. They now sit in "Reading the numbers", which owns
  `RefinementResult.identifiability`, together with `ExchangeRow` and a warning
  that says how the two blocks differ. No name is un-frozen; three moved chapter.
- **"Reading the numbers" gains three sections.** The whole of
  `PatternDiagnostics` and `ContaminationFlag` beside the `diagnose` call it
  already spelled — with the measured warning that an empty contamination list
  means "nothing flagged **or** nothing checked", because a synchrotron
  wavelength has no anode and the screen is skipped silently. The result-side
  identifiability block above. And `AbsorptionCorrection`, which is the worked
  example of this package's rule that a correction ships with a record field
  rather than an Rwp comparison: the capillary factor provably cannot move Rwp,
  so `AbsorptionCorrection.equivalent_delta_biso` is the whole of what it
  changed.
- **The plan presets and the log line freeze where they already lived.**
  "How a refinement works" lists the seven `RefinementPlan` constructors and
  always did; only the `rx.` prefix stood between them and the coverage guard.
  "Files and projects" gains `HistoryRecord`, the three-way tagged union that is
  one line of `history.jsonl`, and why an annotation is its own line rather than
  a field on the node it tags.
- **A stage-report example that would have raised.** "Running a refinement"
  printed `report.statistics.rwp` over `Refinement.stage_reports_`. Those are
  `StageReport` objects, which carry `StageReport.rwp` directly and have no
  `statistics`. The block was marked as not executed, which is the class of
  error that marker hides.
- **New chapter: "The command line".** The five `rietx` subcommands, which no
  page had covered: `index`, whose exit status is a contract a shell pipeline
  can branch on — 0 when a cell reached the confidence gate, 1 when the result
  abstains — with its options, its `--ceiling` cost answer and a worked
  abstention on certified corundum where the right cell ranks first and the
  command still exits 1; `watch`; `html`; `compare`, including that its
  standards are test data and need `--data` pointing at a checkout; and `gui`
  in one **beta**-marked section. It freezes nothing: the entry point is a
  console script and no `rietx.cli` name is on the public surface.
- **The trend fit was documented as joint and is not.** `report.layer1._trends`
  fits each angular template **alone**, as a through-origin nested model,
  because a joint fit of collinear templates returns absurd amplitudes — a
  0.02° zero-point error came back as a 1.8° `constant` cancelled by a −1.8°
  `cos_theta`. Its own docstring claimed the joint fit; its inline comment
  twelve lines down said the opposite. The behaviour was always the second one.
  `TrendAnalysis.separability_ratio` and `TrendTemplate.r2` are described
  exactly in the new chapter as a result.
- **A caveat that was documented as having one cause.** `not_validated` was
  described, in the schema comment and in "Indexing an unknown cell", as "no
  pattern was supplied". It fires whenever *that candidate* has no Le Bail fit,
  and the other cause is a run whose ceiling expired part-way down the
  shortlist, which `INDEX_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` counts. Measured under a 45-second
  ceiling, `IndexingResult.validated` is `true` while every candidate reads
  `not_validated`, so the run-level flag and the per-candidate caveat are not
  each other's negation. The behaviour is unchanged; the description was wrong
  in both places.
- **A result row no longer asserts what nothing measured.** `RefinedParameter`
  declared two fields the refinement path never wrote. `at_bound` was
  `bool = False`, which reads as "this parameter is not against a bound" — an
  answer, given about a parameter no code had looked at. It is now
  `bool | None`, populated from the same findings the `BOUND_HIT` diagnostic is
  built from, with `None` where the row was not tested: a tied row, which is
  not in the free vector, and any result built without a fit behind it, such as
  `replay`. `initial` is removed; the start of a fit lives one node up in the
  history tree, which is the one authority for it.
  `RefinementResult.correlation_warnings` is removed for the same reason —
  nothing wrote it, so it serialised as an empty list that read as "no
  correlations were flagged", while the correlations themselves are reported as
  `HIGH_CORRELATION` diagnostics and on `RefinementResult.identifiability`.
  `TieSpec.from_tie` becomes private: its argument is a type that is not
  public, so as a public classmethod it was a converter that could not name
  what it converts.
- **Two closed vocabularies dropped a member no code produced.**
  `StageResult.status` and `NodeMetrics.status` admitted a fourth value,
  `"skipped"`, that nothing anywhere set: the solver terminates three ways, and
  a stage a plan does not run produces no `StageResult` at all. `NodeKind`
  admitted `"lebail_update"` for a node no verb commits — a Le Bail stage
  refreshes its intensities inside itself, and the refreshed values ride on
  that stage's node. The second had consumers already written against it:
  `NodeAction.api_call` rendered `ref.lebail_update(…)`, **a call that does not
  exist**, and the GUI's history panel had a label for it. Both are gone, and
  every member of both vocabularies now names something the package does.
- **`BACKEND_UNAVAILABLE` can fire, and no longer fires on the wrong thing.**
  The code is documented as "a valid backend name whose optional dependency is
  not installed", and it never produced that answer: `resolve_backend` raises
  `ImportError`, so a `backend="jax"` request on a build without jax came back
  `REFINEMENT_FAILED` with the generic "the physics refused" advice. The
  exception the envelope did map to it, `NotImplementedError`, is raised by
  unsupported *feature* paths — so a soft-restrained `refine_multi` on
  `backend="numpy"` was told to install jax. The check now happens **before
  dispatch**, from `BackendCapability.available`, which is the same answer
  `capabilities()` publishes; an unsupported feature falls to
  `REFINEMENT_FAILED` with the engine's own message. The three-value vocabulary
  is unchanged.
- **A series table no longer has two columns called `index`.**
  `SeriesResult.to_table` named its axis column after `SeriesResult.x_label`,
  which defaults to `"index"` — the name the first column already had. The axis
  column now falls back to `x` when its label is already taken. Column count,
  order and meaning are unchanged.
- **The report contract gained two placements, versioned 1.2 and 1.3**
  (`report_thresholds_version`; both additive, no number moves). 1.2:
  `SuggestedAction.execution` carries the recipe table's `how`
  (`"stage"` / `"index"` / `"advice"`) on every emitted action, so a JSON
  consumer no longer needs the prose to know that an advice action's empty
  `parameter_paths` is by design; and the two action kinds that had never
  been emitted anywhere earned writers, `refine_profile_widths` and
  `collect_better_data`, both by measurement. 1.3:
  `Statistics.identifiability_clause` delivers the report summary's
  identifiability sentence — the exchange finding, the swap experiment and
  what its outcome licenses — verbatim inside `result.statistics`, because
  measured agent consumers pipe the JSON response to a file and grep the
  statistics back, and the summary string is what those greps drop. Written
  by `build_report`, never by the fit: `None` until a report is built, and
  `None` when nothing crossed a comment threshold. The summary keeps its
  copy — one render, two writes, pinned bit-identical.
- **The extinction screen no longer refutes a class on its neighbour's tail.**
  On the certified corundum pattern (NIST SRM 676a, α-Al₂O₃, `R -3 c`)
  `determine_extinction_symbol` returned `R - - -`, a class whose five members do
  not include the certified group at all. Both positions it refuted `R - c -` on
  sit on the **low-angle flank** of a strong allowed line, 2.8 and 1.5 FWHM out;
  measured at the same offsets, sham positions carrying no reflection of any kind
  clear the same 3σ test on 40–50 % of probes and reach 24.7σ, one-sided, which
  is the unmodelled axial-divergence tail rather than any absence. Freeing the
  FCJ asymmetry improves the fit and does not remove the refutation, so a better
  profile is not the repair. `ExtinctionCandidate.n_testable` gains a third
  clause: a forbidden position counts only where the class's **own fitted
  pattern** leaves that window below the test's own detection threshold, so no
  error in a neighbour's tail — not even a total one — can manufacture a
  refutation. The screen now returns `R - c -` = {`R 3 c`, `R -3 c`} at ΔBIC
  −218 with five testable positions all absent. Refutation is still one-sided
  and still outranks ΔBIC wherever a testable position does carry intensity, and
  `ExtinctionScreen.profile_rwp` is still the field to read first: over the whole
  5–150° range with widths a third too wide, this same specimen is still refuted.
- **Indexing is declared provisional, so its chapter documents it without
  freezing it.** The entry above is the reason: a call frozen by the chapter
  that documented it had to change what it answers three weeks later, and the
  subsystem's engines, gates and figures of merit are still being measured
  against real data. Documenting it was never the alternative — a subsystem
  under development is the one a reader most needs a reference for — so the
  compatibility promise gains a fourth entry under **Provisional by
  declaration**, and the indexing chapter opens with a banner pointing at it.
  The declaration is by **module**, not by a list of names: everything defined
  under `rietx.indexing` or `rietx.schemas.indexing` inherits it, including a
  type written after this release. What is *not* covered is stated with it —
  `Capabilities.indexing_thresholds_version` and the agent envelope's
  `indexing` arm are versioned contracts and keep their hard freeze. § The
  freeze has the count.
- **The backward chain is reachable from `refine_sequential`.** With
  `direction="both"` the reverse trajectory existed only on
  `SequentialRefinement.backward_`, so a caller of the one-shot functional API
  received the `SEQUENTIAL_PATH_DEPENDENT` diagnostics with no way to look at
  the trajectory they were about. `SeriesResult.backward` is that chain's own
  `SeriesResult`, and `SeriesResult.n_iterations` now says in its own docstring
  that it counts the reported chain rather than the run.

## The freeze

**1044 names left the provisional bucket**, which is what a Part 1 chapter
does: [compatibility](https://yue-here.github.io/rietx/using/compatibility.html)
promises that a name Part 1 documents is frozen from the release that documents
it. Most of them are now frozen. Indexing is the exception, and it is new here:
that subsystem is **declared** provisional, so its names are documented and
still free to move. Chapter by chapter, that is where they went.

The patterns-and-structures chapter freezes 170. The whole of `Parameter`,
`PatternData`, `Cell`, `Atom`, `AnisoU`,
`PreferredOrientation`, `StephensStrain`, `Source`, `EmissionLine`,
`Dispersion`, `ProfileTCHZ`, the three background models and the two surface
roughness models is now frozen, along with the remaining fields of `Structure`,
`Phase`, `Instrument` and `Geometry`.

The parameter-table chapter freezes 29 more: the whole of `ParameterRow`,
`RefinedParameter` apart from `initial` and `at_bound`, `TieSpec` apart from
`TieSpec.from_tie`, `Refinement.parameters`, `Refinement.set_vary`,
`Refinement.set_values`, `Refinement.structure`, `Refinement.instrument` and
`RefinementResult.parameters`.

The refining chapter freezes 71 more: the whole of `Stage`, `StageResult`,
`StageSpec`, `PlanSpec`, `PlanInfo`, `GuardFinding`, `Provenance` and
`RefinementCancelled`, plus `PLAN_INFO`, `PLAN_PRESETS`,
`RefinementPlan.correlation_guard`, `Refinement.run_stage`,
`Refinement.snapshot`, `Refinement.result_`, `Refinement.stage_reports_` and
`RefinementResult.stages`, `.provenance` and `.mode`.

The history and project chapters freeze 117 more, and eleven types are now
frozen in full: `RefinementTree`, `HistoryNode`, `NodeAction`, `NodeMetrics`,
`RefinementState`, `ReflectionState`, `TreeHeader`, `Annotation`, `Project`,
`ProjectDoc` and `DataRef`. With them go `replay`, `Refinement.branch`,
`Refinement.merge`, `Refinement.cherry_pick`, `Refinement.from_node`,
`RefinementResult.node_id` and `RefinementResult.tree_id`.

The indexing chapter documents the subsystem in full — **257 names** — and
freezes none of them. Indexing is still under active development, so it is
declared provisional as a whole instead: `pick_peaks`, `index_pattern`,
`determine_extinction_symbol` and everything else under `rietx.indexing`, plus
the answer types in `rietx.schemas.indexing` — `PeakList`, `ObservedPeak`,
`DataQualityReport`, `ShiftScreen`, `ShiftTemplateFit`, `ReflectionPairScreen`,
`SearchSpecSpec`, `IndexingControls`, `IndexingResult`, `CellCandidate`,
`FigureOfMerit`, `BravaisOpinion`, `AmbiguityPartner`, `LeBailValidation`,
`IndexingEvidence`, `CandidateEvidence`, `CaveatEvidence`, `ExtinctionScreen`
and `ExtinctionCandidate`. They are documented, they work as they stand, and
they may change in a 1.x release. The capability names that chapter also
freezes — `EngineCapability`, the rest of `SearchPresetCapability` and
`Capabilities.search_presets` — describe the search rather than being part of
it, and they are the nine-name difference between the 257 above and the 266
this section attributed to the chapter before.

**This release is the argument for the declaration.**
`determine_extinction_symbol` was frozen by that chapter the day it landed, and
its answer changed here, because writing the chapter over the call found it
refuting a certified space group. The engines, the gates and the figures of
merit are being measured against real data the same way. Declaring the
subsystem is what stops the next one being a surprise, and what a reader gets
in exchange is stated with it: every change is in the release notes, and the
contracts a consumer parses — `Capabilities.indexing_thresholds_version` and
the `indexing` arm of the agent envelope — keep their own hard freeze. The
promise in full is
[Provisional by declaration](https://yue-here.github.io/rietx/using/compatibility.html#provisional-by-declaration).

The series chapter freezes 83 more, and eight types with it: `SeriesResult`,
`SeriesEntry`, `Trajectory`, `SequentialRefinement`, `MultiHistogramRefinement`,
`HistogramResult`, `SharingMap` and `SharingSpec`, together with
`RefinementResult.histograms` and `RefinementResult.for_histogram`.

The phase-analysis and exports chapters freeze 49 more, and four types:
`QuantitativePhaseAnalysis`, `PhaseQuantity`, `MicroabsorptionCorrection` and
`ReflectionRow`. With them go `RefinementResult.qpa`, `RefinementResult.sig`,
`RefinementResult.sigma`, `RefinementResult.ticks`, `Refinement.predict`,
`Refinement.reflection_table`, the three `Refinement.write_*` methods,
`reflection_table` and `format_su`.

The agent chapter freezes 101 more, and thirteen types with it: the five request
types, `AgentSuccess`, `AgentFailure`, `AgentError`, `AgentErrorDetail`,
`BackendCapability`, `AnodeCapability`, `ReaderCapability` and
`ReaderOptionCapability` are now frozen in full, and so is `Capabilities`
itself. `SuggestionResult` is the hundred-and-first: the chapter names it as the
type of the `suggestion` arm, which freezes the name while its own fields stay
provisional for the report chapter.

The report chapter and the three small groups beside it freeze the remaining
158, and twenty-six types are now frozen in full: `Region`, `UnmatchedPeak`,
`RegionAttribution`, `BasisCoefficient`, `TrendAnalysis`, `TrendTemplate`,
`TextureAnalysis`, `StrainAnalysis`, `BackgroundEvidence`,
`IdentifiabilityEvidence`, `ExchangeFinding`, `LeBailGap`, `SuggestedAction`,
`StageReport`, `SuggestionResult`, `CandidateGroup`, `ParameterCandidate`,
`Identifiability`, `CorrelationPair`, `SoftMode`, `ExchangeRow`,
`PatternDiagnostics`, `ContaminationFlag`, `AbsorptionCorrection`,
`RefinementPlan` and `HistoryRecord`. With them go
`RefinementResult.identifiability` and `RefinementResult.absorption`.

The names left out were left out for one reason: a chapter freezes what it
documents, so a name it cannot describe honestly stays provisional rather than
earning a passing mention. Four were left, and they were the whole of the
provisional tier — three fields nothing in the package wrote, and a converter
for a type that is not public. Rather than being frozen as they stood, three of
them are gone and the fourth answers a real question; the entry in § What
changed has each decision. **`RefinedParameter.at_bound` is the last name in
the deferred bucket**, frozen here by the parameter-table chapter's account of
its three states, and the bucket is now empty.

**The surface itself grew by 24 names, and none of them are new API.** A
dataclass field with no default is never assigned on the class, so the
derivation that enumerates the public surface could not see `Stage.name`,
`Stage.turn_on`, the four fields of `GuardFinding` and `PlanInfo`, eleven of
`ReflectionRow`, and six more — while counting the defaulted fields beside
them. They were public all along and simply absent from the count. The
enumeration now reads dataclass fields explicitly; the effect on a user is that
the denominator is honest, and 24 names that were never in either bucket are
now in one.

Build on the frozen names. The surface measures **1320** names, of which
**1063 are frozen and 257 are provisional by declaration** — the indexing
subsystem, all of it. The deferred bucket is empty, so there is no longer a
third answer to "is this name frozen?": it is frozen, or it is indexing. A new
public name now fails the coverage partition on arrival instead of landing
quietly in a bucket, and a new name written in `rietx.indexing` or
`rietx.schemas.indexing` is provisional the moment it exists rather than
whenever someone remembers to say so.

## Upgrading

**Breaking, and this is the first breaking entry 1.0.x has had.**
`Capabilities.schema_version` moves **0.1 → 0.2**, once, for the two field
removals and the `n_testable` change — under
[compatibility](https://yue-here.github.io/rietx/using/compatibility.html)
a removal is a breaking event and each moves its contract's version as one.

Who has to do something:

- **You read `RefinedParameter.initial` or
  `RefinementResult.correlation_warnings`.** Neither was ever written, so
  whatever you read was `None` or `[]`. Delete the code. For a fit's starting
  values, use the history tree — the node above the stage holds the state it
  started from. For correlations, read the `HIGH_CORRELATION` diagnostics or
  `RefinementResult.identifiability`.
- **You load a result JSON written by 1.0.0 or 1.0.1**, with
  `RefinementResult.model_validate_json` or `rietx read`. The schemas are
  `extra="forbid"`, so an older file's `"initial"` and `"correlation_warnings"`
  keys are now rejected rather than ignored. Strip the two keys, or re-run the
  fit. A history tree is unaffected: it stores state, not results, and nothing
  in the package gates on the version stamp.
- **You call `TieSpec.from_tie`.** It is now `TieSpec._from_tie` and private.
  A `TieSpec` still arrives ready-made on `ParameterRow.tie`.
- **You branch on `at_bound`.** It is now three-valued. `if row.at_bound:`
  still means what it meant; `if not row.at_bound:` no longer does, because
  `None` is not the same claim as `False`. Compare against `is True` / `is
  False` if the distinction matters, which on a tied row it does.
- **You match exhaustively on `StageResult.status`, `NodeMetrics.status` or
  `NodeAction.kind`.** Delete the `"skipped"` and `"lebail_update"` branches.
  No writer ever produced either, so no branch you delete was ever taken.
- **You branch on `AgentError.code`.** A missing optional backend now returns
  `BACKEND_UNAVAILABLE` where it returned `REFINEMENT_FAILED`, and an
  unsupported feature combination returns `REFINEMENT_FAILED` where it returned
  `BACKEND_UNAVAILABLE`. Both are corrections toward what the codes are
  documented to mean.
- **You read a series table by column name.** The default header's third column
  is `x` rather than a second `index`. A reader keying by position is
  unaffected; one keying by name was reading pandas' `index.1`.
- **You read `ExtinctionCandidate.n_testable`.** It is now `int | None`, and
  `None` until `ExtinctionCandidate.screened` — the count needs that class's own
  fit, so before it the answer is unknown rather than zero. `if not n_testable:`
  no longer separates "the data can test none of these" from "nobody asked";
  compare against `0`, or read `screened` first. The count itself is also
  smaller on data where a forbidden position falls under a neighbour's tail,
  which is the entry above.
- **You act on an `ExtinctionScreen`.** The screen's *answer* can change on such
  data — a class it refuted may now survive and win. Nothing else in the package
  reads it, so a pipeline that stops at `index_pattern` is unaffected.
- **You import an indexing type or call.** Nothing about them changed in this
  release beyond the entry above, but their promise did: they are documented
  and **provisional**, so pin a version if you build on them. Reading an
  answer is unaffected —
  `Capabilities.indexing_thresholds_version` and `refine_json`'s `indexing` arm
  are versioned contracts and move by the same rules as before.

Nothing else. No forward model, solver or statistic changed, and the same
inputs give the same numbers from a refinement.
