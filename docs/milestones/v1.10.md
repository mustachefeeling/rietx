## How v1.10 is getting here

### Parametric equations tested against their data (1325, in flight)

A parametric fit's coefficient esds assume the declared equation. Since
2026-10-14 every parametric joint fit also reports whether its data rejects
that equation, by a score test at the answer (`RefinementResult.parametric_form`,
`PARAMETRIC_FORM_REJECTED`). On the ZrMo₂O₈ ramp it matched a full refit on
all five equations tried, for one Jacobian. It is blind where no pattern sees the
target, which [1963](../wp/1963-a-kernel-over-the-pattern-coordinates.md)
was filed to address. The same WP's #970 fixed the joint hold for a
per-histogram cell its own pattern cannot see. Both are staged in the notes.

The maintainer's rule for equations is that the output shows which values came
from one. Since the seventh session each joint row names the equation that set it
(`RefinedParameter.equations`, schema 0.59), and `PARAMETRIC_TARGET_UNSEEN` names
the patterns where an equation sets a target its pattern cannot see. The verdict
line now gives its p-value and counts the patterns tested against the subset.
An equation can also be evaluated anywhere with its esd
(`RefinementResult.parametric_value`), from the coefficients' covariance the
result now carries (`RefinementResult.parametric_equations`). With several equations
each is now read twice, with the other equations tied and with them freed. On the
ZrMo₂O₈ ramp the freed reading gave the cubic equation's own refit verdict, which
the tied reading had missed. The maintainer chose the statistical practice
for the verdict: the freed reading decides each equation, as a regression tests a
term added last. A joint reading of every equation at once
(`RefinementResult.parametric_form_joint`) catches two equations that absorb each
other's misfit when freed, and names neither.

### The tick follow-ups (1908, closed 2026-10-13, review round 2026-10-15)

WP-1908 closed in v1.9 with each tick placed where the model draws its peak.
Two reviews of that work found the same mistake in other places. The window
that picks which reflections to model now follows the geometry's shift, so a
large displacement at a range edge no longer leaves a real peak out of the
model. It stops at 180°, past which a wider window would hold fewer
reflections. The three tick builders now share one walk
(`CompiledModel.reflection_positions`), and that walk reads the refined
wavelength as the curve does. Before this, refining λ by 0.1 % left a tick at
150° about 0.43° from its peak, from 2·tan θ·Δλ/λ (derived, not measured on a
fit). A geometry with no shift and a fixed
wavelength keeps every position bit for bit. The satellite arm keeps its own
copy of the window, which [1326](../wp/1326-satellites-without-a-moment.md)
inherited.

### Spherical-harmonics texture (1960, closed 2026-10-15)

A phase can now carry a spherical-harmonics texture, the preferred-orientation
correction for a texture with no single axis (Von Dreele 1997). It came out of
the v2 fence on 2026-10-14 because two TOPAS users asked for it. Its
coefficients refine from zero with exact analytic columns. The default driver
keeps the pole density non-negative at every reflection, and
`TEXTURE_NOT_POSITIVE` reports where the series goes negative between them.
The papers settled the sample angle: it is the physical one, 0° on a
Bragg-Brentano plate. TOPAS's reference agrees, and GSAS-II puts it at 90°, so
GSAS-II's coefficients convert by P_l(0). GSAS-II 5.8.4's own functions are not
invariant in −1, 2/m, rhombohedral axes and −31m's n = 3 terms, and its m-3 is
one function short. These are recorded in the WP and the manual, and the
maintainer chose not to report them upstream. A GSAS-II project's texture now
reads in, converted, and reproduces GSAS-II's multipliers to its single
precision. On the IUCr round-robin sample 2, the platy brucite's worst
weighed-fraction error was 2.71 wt % at order 6, against 2.80 under
March-Dollase. The TOPAS import needs TOPAS's own output, because the reference
states no normalisation
([1973](../wp/1973-a-topas-spherical-harmonics-texture-reads-in.md)).
Suggesting an order from the data is
[1974](../wp/1974-the-texture-screen-says-which-order-the-misfit-needs.md).

### A rigid body the data reject is named (1922, closed 2026-10-15)

A caller can ask whether the data want a rigid body to be a different shape.
`Refinement.check_rigid_bodies` refits each body twice from the fitted state.
One arm keeps the body. The other frees its atoms under soft restraints at the
body's own bonds and angles. The body fails when the freed fit wins on both
Hamilton's test at p < 0.001 and ΔBIC > 10, at the residual's effective sample
size. Then `RIGID_BODY_MISFIT` names the template's largest deviations. The
check came from outside as PR #822 (issue #775) and shipped in 1.8.0. The close
removed a second copy of Hamilton's F statistic from the check, so the verdict
and the reported F come from one function. It also compiles a phase's sites
once, where it had compiled them once per body.

### A returned figure survives a zoom and a pickle (1971, closed 2026-10-15)

A caller can zoom a figure a plot returns, and a notebook still shows it
at its designed size. The gutter labels had been placed in data
coordinates, so a zoom to a tenth of the x range made the notebook image
7.7 times wider. A y zoom made it 15 times taller, which the WP had not
reported. The labels were one cause. A left spine bounded in data
coordinates was the other, because matplotlib clips no spine. The labels
now sit at a fraction of the axes width, and a label whose height leaves
the view is hidden. The bounded spines are clipped to the axes' height,
and a tight save honours the clip. A figure now pickles and loads back
to the same pixels, every renderer's included. Two things had stopped
it: the figure class built inside a function, and the intensity axis's
tick formatter, which was a lambda. The default renders are byte-identical
to `main`'s. The stored pixel pin had been skipping since matplotlib
3.11.2 and is re-pinned.

### The indexing figures take a dark palette (1972, closed 2026-10-15)

The four indexing figures now take `style="dark"`, and in a notebook each
follows the frontend's theme, as the pattern figures have since 1970. Their
colours moved into the figure palette as roles, and the light figures keep
their colours. Each new dark hue keeps its light hue and is 0.19 lighter in
OKLab, the mean lift of the three pairs the palette already held. Every
indexing colour clears the 0.13 floor against its own ground. The weakest
are the light amber at 0.218 and the dark background grey at 0.42. A new
test fails on any colour a light figure draws outside the palette. That
blind spot in the pixel test is how these figures were missed in 1970. No
other module draws a matplotlib figure. Six colour constants left
`rietx.viz.indexing`, which the staged notes record.

### The tutorials run in the browser (1966, closed 2026-10-14)

Every tutorial notebook now runs in a browser tab at rietx.org/lite/, with
nothing installed and no account. WP-1917 had parked this because pip
refused rietx under Pyodide. That was one of four gaps. gemmi, spglib and
the compiled kernels cross-compile to WebAssembly with `pyodide-build`, and
the kernels needed no change. The other two gaps were rietx bugs that any
32-bit or JupyterLite user would have hit. A `np.repeat` count was int64,
which wasm32's 32-bit index type refuses. A lock site read `fcntl.flock` off
the empty module JupyterLite puts in its place. Both are fixed, and their
rules are in the root CLAUDE.md. The slowest tutorial takes 91 s in the
browser against 31 s natively. `pages.yml` builds the site from the same
commit as its rietx wheel. Each landing-page row links it as JupyterLite,
ahead of Colab. No CI job runs the browser, and `docs/lite/smoke.mjs` is the
check. Figures that follow a dark theme are
[1970](../wp/1970-a-notebook-figure-follows-the-frontends-theme.md).

### Notebook figures follow the theme (1970, closed 2026-10-14)

A pattern, fit, series or structure figure in a notebook now shows in the
frontend's light or dark theme, and follows a switch without a rerun. The
indexing figures stay light, which is
[1972](../wp/1972-the-indexing-figures-take-a-dark-palette.md). Its output carries the light
PNG and an HTML pair of images. A `<style>` keyed on each frontend's dark
marker picks between them, and a frontend that strips it shows the light
image. The dark image is the light figure recoloured, which matched a
`style="dark"` drawing in every pixel and keeps what a caller adds. A
structure figure is drawn again from its recipe instead. It was checked live
in JupyterLab 4.6.4, JupyterLite 0.8.6, GitHub's viewer and the manual under
furo. The Lite site follows the reader's setting once the newest tag's
figures carry the pair, and stays light before that. VS Code and Colab were
read from source only. A saved figure costs about three times the bytes it
did. [1971](../wp/1971-a-returned-figure-survives-a-zoom-and-a-pickle.md)
holds two older defects of the returned figure that this work found.

### Corrections judged at the Bragg angle (1976, closed 2026-10-15)

WP-1908's review left one finding without an owner. The absorption record
and the roughness regime check read each factor at the angle a peak is
drawn, while the forward model applies it at the Bragg angle. A refined
displacement separates the two by up to 0.26°. Both now read the Bragg
angle, and the roughness messages quote the drawn one, the pattern's own
axis. One `CompiledModel` method yields both angles, so the walk that places
a tick and the one that judges a correction cannot drift apart. On rutile
with a 0.5 mm displacement the reported ΔBiso moves by at most 0.0025 Å².
The roughness fence can change its verdict near its edge. WP-1908's other
open question, Layer 0 reading flank lobes as unindexed peaks, is
[1977](../wp/1977-a-flank-lobe-read-as-an-unindexed-peak.md).

### The manual's rebuild planned (1975, closed 2026-10-16)

Five sessions turned the maintainer's request for a clearer manual into a
plan. The manual will be rebuilt as a new tree beside the old one, with six
tabs (Start, Guides, Concepts, Reference, Theory, Switching) and three
audience cards on the front page. It will be served per version at
`rietx.org/docs/`, written in Microsoft's style with the repo's rules on top,

code and validation. A spike built the same three pages in Sphinx with

switcher, a dev banner and a build that fails on a warning, and it keeps
every existing guard. Its markdown twin still needs a small writer for four
node types. The work is filed as
[1978](../wp/1978-the-new-manual-tree-and-its-page-contract.md) to
[1986](../wp/1986-guides-and-theory-move-over-and-the-old-tree-retires.md).

research brief and the spike's sources stay in
[the WP](../wp/1975-manual-rebuild-findings.md).

### The new manual's tree stands beside the old (1978, closed 2026-10-16)

The rebuild has somewhere to write. `docs/docs/` builds under
pydata-sphinx-theme with the six tabs and the three audience cards, each tab
a one-paragraph stub. The two trees share one module for the fenced
constants, the citation style and the `{source}` role, so a retuned
threshold reaches both. Every page of the new tree is held to a page
contract by test: an answer sentence under its title, at most 50 000
characters, and no section opening on "as above". A link from Concepts or
Guides into Theory has to land on a built section. The old tree's register,
trail and name-resolution guards read the new tree too. Deployment, the
markdown twin and every page's content stay with
[1979](../wp/1979-the-manual-at-docs-one-tree-per-version.md) to
[1986](../wp/1986-guides-and-theory-move-over-and-the-old-tree-retires.md).
