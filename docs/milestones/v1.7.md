## How v1.7 is getting here

### A foreign file states the atom rietx computed (1527, closed 2026-10-04)

A contributor ran rietx's GSAS-II, GSAS and FullProf files through the real
programs and found five ways a site became another atom: GSAS-II read `7Li`
as hydrogen and `Cu+` as carbon, FullProf stopped on `Zr4+`, and the GSAS
reader made `NI+2_58` a +258 ion. rietx's own round trips passed every case,
because each reader accepted its own writer's spelling. The four fix PRs
landed, and the maintainer then set one rule for every writer: write the atom
rietx computed, in the other program's spelling, and say so where rietx had
to substitute a neutral atom. Applying it turned up a bigger bug: `Na+`,
`Cl-`, `Cu+` and every one-charge label without its digit had computed as
the neutral atom through 1.6, though the table holds each ion, and pymatgen
writes this spelling. Y³⁺, the one ion of *International Tables*' 111 the
table lacked, now comes from ITC Table 6.1.1.4, and past 2.149 Å⁻¹, where
that fit meets neutral Y, it computes as neutral Y, as ITC advises. Each
writer is now tested against the other program's measured reading rule, not
against rietx's reader.

### The `.inp` grammar the reader refused (1433, closed 2026-10-03)

`read_topas_inp` reads the two constructs it refused by name. A phase opened
by `STR(sg)` or `STR(sg, name)` builds, and `#if`, `#elseif` and `#out` over
`#prm` parameters choose the branch TOPAS would read. It is a special case,
not a macro engine: the reference documents `STR(sg)` by its effect, which is
all a clean-room reader may use, and every macro-opened phase in the archive
is that one macro. Over the archive the reader opens 390 files where it opened
385. Four of them stop at rigid bodies, which are WP-1514's. The work is an
outside contributor's (PR #587) and shipped in 1.6.0.

### The solve cost as a quadratic form (1902, closed 2026-10-03)

The structure-solution route's first piece. `solve/cost.py` turns a Le Bail or
Pawley extraction into a quadratic form in the reflection intensities, so a
structure trial is scored without evaluating the pattern: Ω·I matched
`evaluate` to 2.8e-16 on NAC and 3.6e-16 on FAP. The doublet spike came first
and showed multi-line data needs no refusal. It refuses by name what the form
cannot carry: a magnetic phase, a nonlinear term, satellites, a reflection
only a λ/n line reaches, and an extraction the package calls unusable. The
work is an outside contributor's (PRs #580, #648); the last rule landed in a
cleanup session.

### Magnetic interchange (1328, closed 2026-10-03)

A magnetic structure now travels in and out. magCIF reads with the nuclear
positions refined under the highest tabulated group the file's atoms satisfy,
the parent first, and says which it took (`CIF_MAGNETIC_NUCLEAR_SETTING`,
with a `nuclear_group` override). The FullProf, GSAS, GSAS-II and TOPAS
readers that refused a magnetic phase read it, and the writers carry it or
refuse by name rather than write a nuclear phase in silence (#470, #567). The
work landed from an outside contributor in PRs #544 and #571 and shipped in
1.6.0. It is one rung of the [magnetic](magnetic.md) milestone, whose
acceptance is measured when the milestone completes.

### An authored refusal, not a raw traceback (1337, closed 2026-10-03)

Two failures an outside campaign found (#244, #246) now fail in words. A
`tie()` whose implied value leaves a declared bound refuses as the other tie
refusals do: it names the tie, the value, the atom and the bound, and
registers nothing. A Pawley `summary()` and `suggest()` no longer die in a
numpy broadcast. The contributor's PR #537 also fixed a state bug nobody had
reported, a failed `tie()` leaving its tie registered. It shipped in 1.6.0.

### The Le Bail alternation has a stop rule (1323, closed 2026-10-03)

A Le Bail fit is a loop of partition and refinement, and the skill used to
send every Le Bail job to a hand loop with no cap. `fit` now runs the loop
itself under `RefinementPlan.lebail_passes`. It keeps the best pass, stops
when a pass comes back worse or at a fixed point, and names which pass it kept
with each pass's Rwp in `LEBAIL_ALTERNATION_STOPPED`. On issue #210's shapes
the exact cell keeps pass 1, a 0.3 % error converges, and a 2 % error wanders
and the best pass is kept. A run of passes is one job to `rietx watch` and the
GUI, a cancel part-way leaves the best pass standing, and the cap survives the
`.rxt` document. It shipped in 1.6.0. A background left at a seed that is too
low lowers Rwp, so no Rwp rule can see it; that is WP-1542.

### 1.6.0 cut and published (1541, closed 2026-10-03)

The first release numbered at its cut. It carried 217 pull requests, completed
no milestone, and shipped the magnetic rungs that had landed with what is
missing said in the notes and on the compatibility page. The cut wrote the

defects, and figures across the tree quoting esds that #674 had moved with

re-run are named in 1541's handover.

### The skill's evaluation strategy, verified on a pilot (1904, closed 2026-10-04)

Off the milestone. [1904](../wp/1904-the-skill-evaluation-strategy.md)
answered how the agent skill is to be measured, rewritten and kept in sync,
and tried the answer before filing it. The harness is Claude Code's own
`claude plugin eval`, run over a plugin built from the skill tree: two cases
from the repository's own episodes scored with free trace graders and a
rubric, both arms, for under a dollar a case. An audit put the body at 8 700
tokens with rules at 43 % of its bytes and 8 kB restated in the references;
a prototype cut it to 5 300 tokens and passed every gate. The pilot refuted
two assumptions on the way: the existing judging episode is a ceiling on
Sonnet, and a refinement with the skill does more work than one without it,
which decided half the cells by wall clock rather than by the answer. The
work is 1905 (the suite), 1906 (the rewrite) and 1907 (constants, formulas
and a changed-surface check at review).
