# v1.1 — refinement speed

**Opened 2026-08-20** (version → `1.1.0.dev0`; the opening repairs WP-1109's
first session, which landed code while this file still said "not yet
opened"). The milestone was retitled at the opening: it was drafted as
"free-standing peaks", but the 2026-08-20 review of
[WP-1109](../wp/1109-refinement-speed.md) made performance the
competitiveness priority, so the peaks set shifted to v1.2
([../ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md) § v1.2) and this slot took the speed series.
The 1104–1108 appendices below predate the retitle and stay — they are
11xx-block work and this file is their record.

## Scope

A lab-data multi-phase refinement costs seconds rather than minutes, and the
in-situ series workflow becomes TOPAS-competitive. The trigger: a real agent
session (68-pattern series, 4 phases of ZrMo₂O₈, Cu Kα) spent 3 h 20 min
inside refinements that TOPAS fits in well under a second per pattern. The
series, in execution order (`../ROADMAP.md` § v1.1 — refinement speed):
[1109](../wp/1109-refinement-speed.md) exact wins →
[1111](../wp/1111-benchmark-harness.md) benchmark harness →
[1112](../wp/1112-batched-derivative-bases.md) batched Jacobian path +
η-aware windows → [1113](../wp/1113-evaluation-count.md) evaluation count →
[1114](../wp/1114-peaks-buffer-spike.md) peaks-buffer spike →
[1115](../wp/1115-compiled-kernel-spike.md) compiled tier, gated on the
others' measured outcomes. The three-factor decomposition behind that order
(per-evaluation cost × evaluation count × the algorithmic tier) is 1109's
Context; the two Coelho (2018) references are its § References.

## Acceptance

Judged on the WP-1111 harness — wall clock quoted as a **range**, best-of-3
on an idle machine, with venv and platform, per CLAUDE.md § Commands; the
equivalence bar (bit-identity, shift/esd identity, or a recorded re-baseline)
stated per landed WP; never an Rwp comparison.

- **Warm-started series** (the trigger workflow): per-pattern wall in the
  ~1 s band on the harness's series case. **Met, on the maintainer's judgement
  (2026-08-23), and the row is recorded as mis-specified rather than cleared.**
  WP-1124 showed the ~1 s band describes a distribution this case does not
  have, and required the row be judged on the per-pattern table rather than on
  an average. On that table, measured at ship (§ Appendix — acceptance at
  ship): **0.88-2.33 s (median 2.02)** for seven of the nine warm patterns,
  **10.53 s** for pattern 6 and **20.26 s** for pattern 1, against an opening
  baseline of 4.5-22.4 s for *every* warm pattern. The workflow's typical
  pattern is in band. The two that are not are one named mode rather than a gap
  in the work: pattern 6 is a **bounded** discovery — WP-1127 took it from
  18.7 s, and a bounded discovery is still a discovery — and pattern 1 is the
  *first* warm pattern, so the chain has no converged first rung behind it and
  bounds nothing, which is the rule working as designed rather than failing.
  What is left of the front is the `refit=` choice, unowned and
  adaptive-or-nothing; it is carried past this milestone rather than counted
  against this row.
- **Cold trigger-shaped fit** (4 phases, large cells, Cu Kα, ~1000+
  (line, reflection) pairs): low single-digit seconds. **Met**: **5.69-5.72 s**
  against the opening baseline's 50.11-50.43 s, **8.8×**.
- **Stretch, tracked not gating**: cold fit under 1 s — **not reached, and
  measured unreachable on this milestone's terms** (reported either way, as
  this bullet asked). [1114](../wp/1114-peaks-buffer-spike.md) said no on
  numpy and [1122](../wp/1122-compiled-peaks-buffer.md) re-took it on the
  compiled tier, where the buffer does pay on FCJ-dense families (1.9-2.8× on
  the plane seam) — but the plane seam is 54.9 % of the cold fit, so a seam of
  *zero* cost still leaves 2.56 s. Under 1 s was never reachable through this
  route, whatever the buffer's own ratio. The buffer is deferred to ship with
  FPA, which is what raises the images-per-point multiplier the break-even is
  in (root CLAUDE.md § Backends).
- The opening baseline (the "before" every row above is judged against) is
  the [WP-1111 appendix](#appendix--the-opening-baseline-wp-1111-2026-08-20)
  below. Run it with `.venv/bin/python examples/bench_refinement.py`; a later
  WP quotes that table rather than re-deriving one.

## How v1.1 is getting here — the rolling narrative

### 2026-08-22 — WP-1127

**2026-08-22 — the ladder's first rung is a bet, and it was being sized like an
answer (WP-1127).** The front WP-1124 left behind, and the first item off the
solver survey's speed side to ship anything.

WP-1124's decomposition said 57 % of the warm-series wall is first ladder rungs
that get discarded, and that `refit="stages"` runs the trigger chain 1.61×
faster than the shipped `refit="single"` default while WP-0505's small-cell
series measured the opposite. The obvious successor was to *choose* the rung
adaptively. **The per-pattern table refuted that before anything was built**:
the collapse is still the better rung on **seven of the nine** warm patterns
even on the trigger case (0.89-2.35 s against 2.97-4.25 s staged). Its
escalations are sporadic, not systematic, so a rule generalising from one
failure to the rest of the chain pays for the generalisation on every pattern
that did not need it. `refit="stages"` wins that case by never paying for a
discovery, not by fitting better.

So the lever is the **discovery cost**, not the choice. A failed first rung does
not cost more than a successful one; it costs *the whole budget* —
`_collapse` takes `max_iter` as the maximum over the plan's stages, and a losing
bet then spends the plan's largest budget on the widest Jacobian in it. Measured,
a winning first rung and a losing one do not overlap anywhere: **27-64**
evaluations on `trigger-series` and **25-107** on `cpd-series` against **~400**
for both failures, a factor of six between the largest success and the smallest
failure. That separation is what makes a budget derived from the chain's own
history a rule rather than a knob.

`first_rung_factor` (default `FIRST_RUNG_FACTOR = 3.0`) bounds the first rung at
three times the dearest first rung the chain has already **converged**, once
`FIRST_RUNG_SAMPLES = 3` of them are in hand. **`trigger-series` 1603 → 1395
evaluations** (1315 → 1128 Jacobians, 57.3-57.5 → 49.2-49.3 s); `cpd-series`
identical to the evaluation, which is what a bound derived from the cost of
working rungs does on a chain where they all work.

**The answers are bit-identical** — 0 of 1030 and 0 of 392 accepted values differ
at all, not merely inside the 0.25 esd the kill criterion asked for — and the
mechanism is why the bound is safe rather than merely measured: it only truncates
a rung whose result is discarded, and the rung replacing it starts from the warm
state, which the truncation never touched. Two clauses were needed to keep it
that way, and both were found by tests rather than by the harness: spending the
bound must **force** the escalation (`_reseed_needed` tests neither `max_iter`
nor the budget, so the fence would otherwise keep a truncated fit at a good Rwp),
and `_prefer` must rank a truncated attempt below any that completed (`_better`
at equal Rwp keeps the earlier one, so the bound could otherwise pick the answer).

**Two negative results are worth more than the ratio.** The bound was designed
with a second half that sounds better than the one that shipped: *a warm refit
costing more than the cold fit it started from is not a warm refit*. It needs no
constant, and it is the only bound available to the first warm pattern — one of
the two that escalate here — and with it the case measured 1183 evaluations,
1.36×, beating `refit="stages"`. **It is false.** `_collapse` of a one-stage plan
*is* that plan, so for a short plan the cold fit and the collapsed warm rung are
the same problem from different starting points, and the test suite's own cheap
plan has cold **9** evaluations against warm **14** — a rung cut that was about
to succeed. No factor repairs it: the toy needs ≥ 1.56 × cold to be safe and the
trigger case ≤ 1.0 × cold to be useful. It had survived both real harness cases
only because a multi-stage cold fit sums to several times a collapsed rung, which
is a property of those plans and not of the rule. **A one-parameter toy plan was
a sharper adversary than either real dataset**, which is the opposite of the
usual expectation.

The second: the WP's pre-registered clause 1 — *"it costs whole-chain evaluations
against the better of the two fixed modes"* — **fires** on the final rule, 1395
against `refit="stages"`'s 1253 (the rule as shipped measures 1395). It is recorded as firing rather than reworded,
because a criterion corrected after the data cannot be told from a moved
goalpost. The judgement beside it is that the clause was written for a rule that
*chooses* the rung, where "worse than picking the right mode" is the hazard, and
the bound chooses nothing: it improves whichever mode the caller picked and makes
no chain worse. The default was flipped on that reading, by the maintainer.

**What is left of this front is the `refit=` choice itself.** 1253 against 1395
says the mode matters more than the bound on the trigger case, and 627 against
1041 says the reverse on the small-cell one. Unowned, and adaptive-or-nothing.

### 2026-08-22 — WP-1125

**2026-08-22 — variable projection cannot help, and the reason is an identity
(WP-1125).**  The second probe the solver survey's re-assessment promoted, and
the second retired the same day.  WP-1113 had named the expensive stages as
ftol-bound Gauss-Newton tails walking a zero ↔ displacement ↔ **background**
degeneracy; the background is linear and already isolated as a design matrix,
so profiling it out looked like removing one leg of that walk by construction.
It removes nothing, because there was never a separate leg to remove.

**The profiled Gauss-Newton step is the joint Gauss-Newton step.**  Minimising
the joint linearised model over the linear block first leaves
min ‖(I − P)·(r + J·Δθ)‖², which is the profiled subproblem exactly — the Schur
complement of the joint normal matrix — and it holds *wherever the block
currently sits*, not only at its conditional optimum, because
(I − P)·M = M − M·M⁺·M = 0 is a Moore-Penrose identity.  So variable projection
asks this solver for the step it was already going to take.  Measured as a
third gate the WP had not asked for: the two unconstrained steps agree to
**≤ 6.6e-07 relative on all 70 stages**, at start-identity gaps spanning
4.5e-15 to 1.2e+03 — which is how the intuition that the equality needs a
converged background got refuted rather than assumed.

The counts follow.  **On the 34 stages where TRF never rejected a step — where
it therefore took the Gauss-Newton step — the accepted-step counts are
identical in 34 of 34**, including every `zero_disp`/`cell` row at both
schedules (12 of 12 at exactly 1.00×, decay ratio equal to three decimals),
with WP-1113's baselines 84/86/93/131 reproducing exactly.  The final stage,
which WP-1123's schedule could not reach and was the likeliest place a win
lived, inherits the walk instead of shedding it: eight `biso` rows spanning
0.54–1.08×, median 0.94×.  Across all 70 stages of five cases the whole probe
is **0.79×** — profiling is *worse*, not neutral.

Worse, because scipy's TRF takes its initial trust radius from ‖x0‖ and
background coefficients are **counts** while everything else is order 1: the
linear block carries nearly the whole norm (9.79e+02 of 9.79e+02 on the
trigger's `profile` stage), so removing it shrinks the starting radius 1.0–59×,
median 3.6×.  That stage is the worst row in the probe — 400 accepted steps
against 13, into the evaluation ceiling, finishing 15.1 % higher in cost and
7.85 esd away, with its Gauss-Newton step still right to 2.5e-09.  This half is
a fact about the driver and a landing WP could fight it with `x_scale`; it is
recorded because it explains the sign, not because it is the verdict.  **The
verdict is the identity, which caps the gain at 1.00× however well the
globaliser is tuned.**

**The yield is a correction to the survey's own reasoning.**  A1 rested on Ruhe
& Wedin — alternation converges linearly, variable projection quadratically —
and that comparison is against **alternation**, which this package has never
done: the background is an ordinary column of θ solved jointly with everything
else.  The quadratic-vs-linear argument never applied to it.  That is now a
root CLAUDE.md rule, because anyone who notices the background is linear will
propose this again.  What E5 still stands on is untouched and unmeasured: the
**correctness** half (the VarPro normal matrix is the Schur complement, so
structural esds emerge marginalised over the background rather than conditional
on it) and the **Pawley dimension** claim, which is per-step linear algebra over
550 stored columns rather than evaluation count over six near-free design rows.
Neither may quote this probe's identity — it lapses the moment a bound goes
active, and both of those blocks are non-negative.

Two smaller things the probe measured in passing.  Its own pre-registered gates
both failed, and neither means what its name suggests: claim 1 (agreement
within 0.1 esd) failed on 10 of 70, every failure on a stage where TRF rejected
steps, so it measures two trust regions diverging rather than a disagreement
about the minimum; claim 2 (the conditional-solution identity at the endpoint)
failed on 34 of 70 at worst 4.0e-03, which is not a defect in the identity but
a fact about the joint fit — **an ftol-bound stage stops with its background up
to 0.4 % from its own conditional optimum.**  And one stage in 70 was
*all-linear*: a Le Bail seed stage frees only the background, so it is one exact
solve against 9 accepted steps.  Exploiting that needs the runner to notice a
stage has no nonlinear parameter, which is a much smaller idea than A1 and is
unscheduled.

Nothing shipped and `src/` was untouched; the deliverable is
`examples/probe_varpro.py`, which was checked against the thing it measures —
the joint arm's per-stage counts are bit-identical with and without the probe
running beside it, and two full runs are byte-identical.

### 2026-08-22 — WP-1124

**2026-08-22 — the warm-series band, decomposed; predictor seeding retired
(WP-1124).**  The milestone's one remaining gating target was "per-pattern wall
in the ~1 s band", measured at ~5.7 s a pattern.  The probe was aimed at the
seed — the solver survey's B8, extrapolating each pattern's start along the
chain's trajectory instead of copying its predecessor's endpoint — and it
closed **negative**.  It is worth more than the verdict, because the target
statement turned out to be describing a distribution it does not have.

**The harness was reporting the cheap half of the expensive patterns.**  A
pattern emits one `fit_start`/`fit_end` pair *per ladder rung*, and
`_run_series` kept the last of each kind — so a pattern that escalated reported
only the rung that succeeded, which is by construction the one cheap enough to
be kept.  Its docstring already claimed the whole escalation, so the fix made
the code match what it said.  Pattern 1 read 3.21 s and had spent **20.19 s**;
pattern 6 read 3.13 s and had spent 18.82 s.  **32.63 s of the 57.45 s series —
57 % — is wall in first rungs the ladder then throws away.**  So ~5.7 s a
pattern is an average over a bimodal distribution and neither mode is at it:
seven clean warm patterns at **0.89-2.32 s**, already at or just above target,
and two at 18.8-20.2 s.  And the shipped default first rung is the slower one
here — `refit="stages"` runs the same chain **1.61×** faster (35.76-35.97 s
against 57.66-57.96) with **zero** escalations, inverting WP-0505's small-cell
measurement (904 collapsed against 1623 staged) in the direction WP-1110's
agent round hit on a real trigger-shaped model.

**Against that, what a predictor bought.**  Both arms — a secant in physical
values and a corrector-checked Gauss-Newton tangent — were built on the
existing `fit(prepare=…)` seam, so nothing shipped.  On `refit="single"` they
cut whole-chain evaluations past a zero-spread baseline (1603 → 1495 and
**1287**, the tangent's 1.25× being the probe's best number) and **the series
wall did not move**: the ranges overlap the copy baseline's.  Each removed one
of the two escalations and made the survivor *more* expensive — the tangent's
pattern 6 burned 26.19 s in its discarded rung against copy's 15.60 s at a
similar iteration count.  A predictor changes *which* pattern's collapsed rung
fails, not whether one does, and the cost of one that does is set by `max_iter`
on a ~30-parameter TRF call, which no seed reaches.  Endpoint agreement was
*not* the problem: outside the exactly degenerate width family WP-1123 named,
the arms sit at 0.105 and 0.047 esd median.

**Three findings that outlast the verdict.**  (i) In the `refit="stages"`
regime — no escalation catastrophe to swamp the effect, so the clean
measurement — both arms cost *more*: 1.16× and **1.67×** evaluations, up to
2.0× wall.  (ii) The tangent walked pattern 6 into a different basin, 941
iterations to Rwp **0.02252** against 0.01950, and because that endpoint is
what the chain carries **every successor inherited it**; `phases.2.cell.b` ends
**186 esd** out, the reseed fence never fired (1.15× is inside `RESEED_FACTOR`)
and the only report was one `SEQUENTIAL_DISCONTINUITY`.  The corrector check
**passed** on that step — it lowered the residual at the warm state and still
led the refit to a worse minimum — so *a cost-decreasing predictor step is not
a safety property*: better by cost, worse by basin, WP-1113's width valley
reached from a new direction.  (iii) The kill criterion's clause that fired:
both arms turned `SEQUENTIAL_PATH_DEPENDENT` from **absent** to reported.  The
mechanism is structural, not an implementation slip — a first-order predictor
needs two converged points, so it cannot seed the first two patterns of a pass,
and the forward chain is seeded on patterns 2-9 against the backward one's 7-0.
**The method is asymmetric in the series coordinate by construction, and
`direction="both"` is exactly the instrument that sees it.**

The front therefore stays open and moves one rank down, from the seed to the
ladder: the lever is *which rung the ladder starts on*, not what the rung
starts from.  That wants its own WP and an adaptive answer rather than a
flipped default, because WP-0505 measured the opposite on small-cell standards.
One method note the series should keep: the same `refit="stages"` copy chain
measured 35.76-35.97 s and 41.77-42.93 s in two processes minutes apart at an
identical 1253 nfev — **1.17× of pure machine state, larger than most of the
effects above** — which is why every arm comparison here is between arms inside
one run.

### 2026-08-22 — WP-1122

**2026-08-22 — the stretch, measured out of reach; the buffer handed to FPA
(WP-1122).**  This is the milestone's one *negative* close, and it is worth
more than a summary of activity: the target was not missed by a bit of
engineering, it was measured unreachable by the route that was supposed to
reach it.  1114 had said no to the peaks buffer on numpy and named the
substrate, not the physics, as the reason; 1115 replaced that substrate; 1122
re-took the verdict there, which is exactly the sequence 1114's own no-go
prescribed.

The re-take says the buffer *works* on the compiled tier: a fused
reconstruction over 1114's own anchors and stored grid runs **1.9-2.8×** ahead
of the shipped exact plane seam on the trigger's four FCJ phases, and agrees
with 1114's numpy prototype to 4.3e-16.  It also says two things that settle
the question against building it now.  The first is **Amdahl**: the plane seam
is 54.9 % of the cold trigger fit (forward 22.1 %, bases 32.8 %, re-measured
here rather than quoted), so a plane seam of zero cost leaves 2.56 s against a
< 1 s stretch — no arrangement of this WP reaches it.  The second is the
**break-even**, which is the transferable result: what decides whether shape
reuse pays is neither the tolerance nor the anchor count but how many FCJ
images a window point carries, and it is **2.78-4.16** across the harness.
The trigger's phases carry 7.79-7.87 and clear it; every other family in the
harness carries exactly **1.00** and loses 3-7×, because below break-even the
anchor build alone costs more exact elements than the whole exact evaluation
(`vol×` 0.26-0.59 on the lab cases).  No tolerance, kernel or anchor count
moves that; it is arithmetic about volume.

Per-family selection would leave the whole harness at **1.48×** on one case of
four and unchanged on the rest — against the package's first approximate
forward mode, two substrates held to stated bars, a declared tolerance and its
whole recording surface.  So the maintainer's call was **do not build it, and
build it with FPA**, where the multiplier the break-even is in is exactly what
FPA raises.  That is a deferral with a trigger rather than a shelf: the design
(1114's note), the accuracy result (K ≤ 32 anchors at 1e-4, every case and
state) and the cost model (1122 § Findings) are all recorded, and the fence is
in root CLAUDE.md § Backends so re-opening it needs a reason.

One caveat is left open and named: the harness holds **no lab case with FCJ
actually on** — `qarr_instrument` leaves both axial ratios at 0.0 and nac is
synchrotron Debye-Scherrer — so where a real Bragg-Brentano fit with declared
axial divergence sits against the break-even is unmeasured.  It decides
whether the buffer would serve one simulated case or a class of real ones, and
it needs a dataset this harness does not have.

Two method rules the probe paid for, both of which flatter the buffer if
missed.  `compiled._spread` engages the pool only above 512 rows and the exact
path splits by FCJ *bucket* while a reconstruction splits by row, so the first
cut compared a threaded buffer against a serial exact path on the one phase
big enough to cross the threshold and read 1.29× where the serial answer is
1.11× — **when benchmarking two paths, force both serial and let threading
cancel out of the ratio.**  And 1114's prototype builds anchors in numpy;
charged that way the trigger phases read 0.53-1.11× against 1.89-2.81× at the
compiled rate — **price each side's scaffolding at the same tier, or the
comparison is between substrates rather than between designs.**

### 2026-08-22 — WP-1123

**2026-08-22 — the tolerance schedule, flipped on (WP-1123).**  WP-1113 named
the mechanism and priced the flip, then landed the knob opt-in; this is the
decision, executed.  Every stage of a staged plan but the last now stops at
``ftol = 1e-6``, the last at the solver's own 1e-9, through one plan-level
field — ``RefinementPlan.intermediate_ftol`` — applied by one authority,
``stage_ftols()``, which is the only object that knows whether a stage is the
last.  ``intermediate_ftol = None`` is the whole way back, bit for bit.

Measured on the merged tree (`[dev]`, darwin/arm64, best of three, idle),
both schedules through the same harness: **cpd-1a 408 → 270** nfev (1.51×,
2.02-2.04 → 1.52 s), **cpd-2 533 → 329** (1.62×, 3.37-3.43 → 2.27-2.28 s),
**trigger 360 → 232** (1.55×, 8.63-8.65 → **5.67-5.70 s**), nac 47 → 39
(0.38 → 0.34-0.35 s).  Rwp agrees to five decimals or better on every case.
1121's closing note predicted "against 8.7 s that is ≈ 5.4 s"; the measurement
is 5.7.

What it costs, measured here rather than quoted: cpd-1a's widest
non-degenerate move is **0.027 esd** (`phases.2.gauss_size`), cpd-2's is
0.001 esd on a background coefficient, the trigger's is 0.001 esd *outside* one
exactly degenerate family — where instrument ``x`` gains 0.0013165 and all four
phases' ``lor_size`` lose 0.0012897-0.0013300 each, so the width they sum to
does not move.  QPA within 0.0014 wt %.  **The bound is a few hundredths of an
esd, not 1113's 0.02**: same schedule, different tree, and it is the tree that
moved it — which is the reason to re-measure rather than inherit a bound.

**The chained series changed sign between two measurements one commit apart,
and that is the finding.**  Before merging 1121 it cost more (1705 evaluations
against 1634); after, it pays 1.12× (1603 against 1792, 57.2-57.6 s against
60.4-60.7 s).  Nothing about the schedule changed.  Each pattern warm-starts
from its predecessor, so a bounded per-fit difference becomes a rung escalation
or a rung avoided and the chain integrates it: unbounded in magnitude, not
fixed in sign.  A single cold fit has no such amplifier and was faster in both
trees.  So the rule that landed in root CLAUDE.md is not "the series is slower"
but "a series is measured, never assumed".

Three recording gaps closed with it, because a default that fires everywhere
makes them universal: ``StageResult.ftol`` (what a stage actually stopped at),
``NodeAction.ftol`` (the tolerance the stage *ran* at, not the one it declared,
or a cherry-pick replays the one thing the run did not do) and
``NodeAction.window_slack_deg`` (the same gap, left by WP-1112).  ``SCHEMA_VERSION``
0.4 → 0.5 — its first entry whose **default** changes an answer rather than only
the field list.  The acceptance suites now name the schedule the way they name
``dispersion``, and ``validation_matrix.INTERMEDIATE_FTOL_DEFAULT`` records the
trade beside the numbers it produced, pinned to the live default by a test.

A crash fell out of the first measurement, and it belonged to the day-old
compiled tier: ``_redirect_cache`` read ``sys.modules['numba'].config``
unguarded while ``warm()`` was still importing numba on its background thread,
so the *first* fit after a fresh install could die with an ``AttributeError``
from inside ``enabled()``.  **Two sessions hit it within hours of each other**
— this one, in a benchmark that died in setup and ran clean on the retry, and
WP-1121's on main, whose fix is the one the merge kept — which says more about
the window than either report alone: it is the import itself, wide while the
package files are cold in the page cache, so it is a *first run after install*
bug and both sessions were installing fresh venvs.  Neither pinned it by
racing; both built the half-imported module and asserted the call survives it.

### 2026-08-21 — WP-1114

**2026-08-21 — the peaks buffer, measured to a no-go (WP-1114).**  The spike
asked TOPAS's question of this code base — can a small number of peak
*shapes* stand in for every (line, reflection) evaluation — and both halves
of the answer are now measured rather than argued.  The physics half says
yes, emphatically: a C² cubic spline through greedily-placed anchor shapes
reproduces every shape in every harness case, at the cold start *and* at
convergence, to 1e-4 on area and both moments with **K ≤ 32 anchors per
distinct width family** (K ≤ 16 at 1e-3), lines shared, and the greedy
probe-and-bisect placement even survives the genuine C¹ kinks the converged
lab states carry (the fitted Caglioti quadratic goes negative mid-range and
`_MIN_GAMMA_G2` clamps Γ_G across 2θ ≈ 92-134° — a fixed anchor grid
plateaus at ~3-5e-4 there forever).  The substrate half says no: a full
prototype — anchor planes, θ-spline, prefiltered B-spline resample, the
Jacobian bases riding the same anchors — holds its tolerance through entire
fits (medians ≤ 0.02 esd; every esd that moved > 5 % is an unmeasured
width-soup direction with esd/|value| ≥ 7e2) yet buys only **~10 % on the
trigger cold fit** (28.5-28.8 → 25.5-26.2 s) and *costs* 2.4× on the cpd-2
protocol, because WP-1112's batched kernel already runs within ~2× of the
memory floor and interpolation trades away arithmetic nobody is paying for.
The spike's most valuable number is one it was not sent for: the *residual*
still runs the scalar per-reflection loop, and evaluating the same forward
through the batched kernel is bit-exact and **2.2-3.6× faster** (17.1→7.5 ms
trigger, 2.9→0.8 ms cpd-2) — opened as WP-1120.  The go/no-go, the
anchors-vs-accuracy curve, the design note (which a compiled substrate could

### 2026-08-22

**2026-08-22 — the residual gets the win the derivative side took a WP
earlier, and the prototype for it could not be lifted.** WP-1120. WP-1112
batched the derivative bases and left the residual on the per-reflection loop;
WP-1114's spike measured what that cost and wrote a reference implementation to
lift. It could not be lifted. `derivative_bases` builds Ω from
`_profile_basis`, the residual from `_profile`, and those two spell u²
differently and land 1-2 ulp apart *by design* — `_profile_basis` exists
precisely so a caller's decision about which partials it wants cannot move the
bases. Lifting the reference would have moved every converged fit in its last
digits, quietly, in exchange for nothing. So what is shared with the bases is
the batched *shape* — layout, buckets, node mix, masking — and the profile
spelling is a parameter of it, with each caller declaring which Ω it is
reproducing.

The result is **1.65× on the trigger cold fit** (28.33-28.44 → 17.07-17.29 s)
and 1.11-1.15× on the four lab and synchrotron cases, with the whole of the
gain inside `evaluate` (11.19 s of 11.41 s) and time elsewhere unchanged.
**Four of the five cases return identical per-stage nfev and identical Rwp**,
which is the equivalence bar met over 47 to 540 evaluations rather than at one
— the symmetric path is bit-identical end to end. The trigger is the only case
carrying FCJ and the only one that moves: a batched matmul replaces a
per-reflection dgemv, worth ~1 ulp, and one ulp is enough to change a
trust-region decision (363 → 364 evaluations) and not enough to change a
parameter or an esd.

Two method notes worth more than the speedup. **A guard that reverses the phase
order proves nothing** about a per-phase scatter: addition is commutative, so
with two phases `P0 + P1` is `P1 + P0` bit for bit. Associativity is the
property at stake, and the gate now builds the regrouped variant — one bincount
across every phase — and asserts it differs. That guard was written, falsified
on purpose, found to pass whatever the code did, and rewritten. **And a
forward's cost at a plan's starting model under-predicts what a fit pays**:
weighting the starting-model ratio by per-stage nfev predicts 3.5-3.7 s saved
against 11.26 s measured. Two explanations for the gap were measured and are
wrong — the Jacobian's whole-model FD fallback fires on *zero* trigger columns,
and a warm `_cached_fcj_nodes` does not flatter the probe (2.23× with the peaks
moving against 2.25× fixed) — so the WP records the gap as unattributed rather
than inventing a mechanism for it.

### 2026-08-22

**2026-08-22 — the compiled tier ships, and it is what a default install
gets.** WP-1115. The spike was gated on the floor still binding, and it did:
17.1 s cold on the trigger case against a low-single-digit target. But the gate
opened on **neither mechanism the WP was written around**. Dispatch had already
been removed by 1112 and 1120 — the surviving kernel calls are 200-400 µs on
10⁵-element planes — and the ragged axis costs an evaluation-weighted 1.11×,
not the ~2× inherited from 1114. What remained were the two things numpy cannot
do at all: **fuse** (a dozen full-size temporaries per profile call, each a
write and a read of a plane that will not fit in registers) and **thread** (the
GIL denies a numpy-level python loop every core but one). So the WP that was
filed as "compile the ragged loop" landed as "fuse the plane arithmetic and run
it on eight threads", and the numbers are

| case | numpy path | compiled |
|---|---|---|
| `trigger` | 17.56-17.83 s | **8.86-8.99 s** |
| `cpd-1a` | 4.22-4.31 s | **2.17-2.21 s** |
| `nac` | 0.54-0.55 s | **0.40-0.41 s** |

with Rwp equal to five decimals in every case. That is the warm-series target
reached and the cold one still missed by about 2×, and the front after it is
not a plane kernel at all: it is the 15 608 perturbed `phase_peaks` a trigger
Jacobian asks for.

**`prange` was the wrong shape twice over**, which is the finding most likely
to be re-hit. It refuses to cache, so its ~1 s recompiles in *every* process —
and it is slower than the alternative. A serial `njit(nogil=True)` kernel
driven from a shared `ThreadPoolExecutor` over row ranges caches like any other
(0.60 s cold, 0.12 s to reload) and reaches 12.3× against numpy where `prange`
managed 11.2×. The pool must be shared: rebuilding one per call costs more than
half the win.

**The prototype's few-ulp result was an artefact of its own transcription, and
the real bar is set by libm.** The two Ω spellings 1120 found differ in exactly
one association — the forward computes `-4ln2·(x/Γ)²`, the bases `((-4ln2)·u)·u`
— and the Lorentzian is *common* to both, because multiplying by a power of two
is exact. Transcribe each faithfully and symmetric rows land on the same doubles
on darwin/arm64, where numba's `math.exp` and numpy's `exp` agree bit for bit;
they miss by ~3e-17 on Linux, which CI said after a round that failed on exactly
the tests this WP had just written the "declare your path" rule about. The
accumulation, which has no library call in it at all, is bit-identical
everywhere. FCJ rows keep 1112's rounding bar: a sequential node sum against a
matmul, ≤ 4e-16.

That platform split has a sharp corollary. **Where libm differs by an ulp, no
magnitude test can distinguish "used the other Ω spelling" from "used the right
one"** — the two gaps are the same size, 2.87e-17 against 5.74e-17 on the
fixture. So the portable guard for the spelling is structural (the two flags
must produce *different* output, which catches a kernel that ignores the
declaration, and was mutation-tested to confirm it), and the identity check runs
only where identity holds. Dressing the magnitude comparison up as portable
would have been the weakest kind of green.

Three things the spike could not have shown, all found by building it.
**Declining while the compile runs is the wrong semantics** — it makes which
path an evaluation took a function of how fast the machine compiled, so the
same script gives different last digits on two runs; one path per process is
worth the few hundred milliseconds. **A test that pins a number must declare
its path**, which is CLAUDE.md's dispersion-default rule meeting a second
default: `test_backend_shim`'s pre-shim goldens now say they are the numpy
expressions, and a new row holds the kernels to the rounding bar against the
same files. And **the disk cache landed beside the source for one commit**,
because numba reads `NUMBA_CACHE_DIR` when it is imported and `available()`
imported it first — a failure that works perfectly on a dev checkout and
silently recompiles in every process wherever `site-packages` is read-only. It
was caught by counting the cached files, not by timing.

The packaging is the user's decision and the shape it forced is worth
recording, because the obvious answer does not exist. Asked for "numba by
default with a `rietx[slow]` for those who cannot", the answer is that **no
extra can subtract a dependency** — extras only add, so "fast by default and
still installable without the compiler" is not expressible as packaging at all.
It is a code property instead: the import is soft and every kernel keeps the
numpy expression behind it, so `--no-deps` or a distro package still fits (a
real fit in a genuinely numba-free venv returns the same Rwp and cell as the
compiled one, esd differing in the last ulp), and `RIETX_COMPILED=0` switches
the tier off in place. Both states are reported by
`capabilities().features`, as two flags rather than one, because "numba imports
here" and "the next residual will use it" can disagree.


### 2026-08-22

**2026-08-22 — the front past the compiled tier, measured; and the cold target
missed with the remainder named.** WP-1121. 1115 shipped the tier and left the
cold trigger fit at 8.9 s against a low-single-digit target, forwarding one

per-reflection scalars no plane kernel reaches. This WP re-measured that on the

`bench_compiled_kernel.py --seams` carried `set_enabled(False)` at module

half-built module rather than racing a thread.

The decomposition holds the WP's prediction to the point. `phase_peaks` was
11.2 % of wall before the tier and is **20.9 %** after it, because its absolute
cost did not move (1.911 → 1.85 s) while everything round it did — 11.0× on the
compiled scatter, ~2× on the two profile kernels, **1.03×** here. And the
reason is the finding that inverts 1115's own. That gate asked whether python
dispatch was the plane kernels' cost and measured it noise: 200–400 µs calls on
~10⁵-element planes, 2–4 ns an element. The per-reflection blocks run over
98–282 reflections a phase, and a `phase_peaks` call in which *every* memo slot
hits still costs **562 ns per reflection**. Same package, same profile physics,
two regimes three orders of magnitude apart in cost per element — so fusion and
threading pay where there is a plane to fuse, and the lever on the reflection
axis is fewer, larger calls or compiled code that does not dispatch at all.

Two changes landed against it, with unlike equivalence bars, which is the
point. The phase **scale** is the one parameter the intensity is exactly linear
in — it enters `base = scale · mult · |F|²` once and every factor after it is
independent of it — so its column is the phase's own contribution over the
scale, and finite-differencing it was rebuilding a structure factor to
rediscover a multiplication. Its bar is **exactness, not bit-identity**: it
agrees to 3.6e-16 with a difference quotient in *physical* space, which has no
truncation error at any step because the function is linear, while the FD it
replaces carried the softplus transform's O(h) curvature and was 4.6e-6 from
the truth. The whole-model FD makes that same error, agreed with the old column

**The width caveat did not reproduce, and that is worth as much as if it had.**
WP-1110's agent hit a `refit="single"` collapse running past 150 s unfinished

per-stage Jacobian *width*. Both rungs are now cases, and on this one the
collapse is the **faster** of the two — 4.46–22.38 s per warm pattern against
19.76–26.15 s staged, at 1 238 nfev against 1 549 — even though its single
stage frees 61 parameters, twice the ~30 the agent's model gave it. What the
two rungs actually differ in here is *shape*: the collapse is fast and spiky
(5.0× spread, one escalation to `warm_staged`), the staged rung is uniformly
slower and steady (1.3× spread, no escalations, every pattern on the same
rung). Totals land within each other's noise because pattern 0 is cold in both
and dominates.

**Why it did not reproduce is the limitation to carry**, and it is a property
of the simulation rather than of the package: the truth model *is* the fitted
model, so a warm start begins a few hundred ppm from the answer with no model
error to fight. The pathology the agent saw needs a collapse that starts wrong
and has one wide stage to recover in — which no self-simulated case can
produce. So this row bounds the claim rather than settling it: on a model with
no systematic misfit the collapse is cheaper at this width, and whether it
survives real model error is **still open**, needing real series data. Until
then neither rung should be quoted as generally cheaper, and WP-0505's "≈3×"
should not be restated without saying which it was measured on.

### Toby (2024) reconciled against §2 and `PLAN_PRESETS`/`PLAN_INFO`

The paper's thesis: no fixed parameter order is correct, because the right
next parameter depends on the data and the current values — so GSAS-II
computes the "worst-fit parameter" (largest |∂χ²/∂p| with same-sign one-sided
derivatives at p ± δ) instead of following a recipe. Read against this
package, difference by difference:

1. **Static presets vs a computed next parameter — already both, adopt by
   cross-citation.** The presets encode the staged discipline;
   `Refinement.suggest()` / `task="suggest"` (WP-1050) is the state-dependent
   answer Toby's mechanism computes — a Gauss-Newton one-parameter gain off
   one analytic Jacobian build, ranking every held parameter by predicted
   Δχ². The gain is Toby's |∂χ²/∂p| with the curvature already divided out,
   so a stiff parameter with a large gradient and nothing to give ranks low
   rather than first. §2 now points at §9c for this.
2. **The two-sided δ test — declined, with the reason on record.** Toby
   brackets the minimum with χ²(p ± δ) to tell real leverage from roundoff,
   because GSAS-II's probe differentiates numerically. This package's
   gradient is analytic (JᵀR), so a converged parameter's one-parameter gain
   is zero by construction rather than by test; the filter answers a
   finite-difference problem the machinery here does not have.
3. **Cell before scale when the cell is off — declined, two reasons.** Toby:
   with partial peak overlap but a far-from-optimal cell, the cell must be
   optimised before the scale can be. Here the stages are *cumulative*, so
   the `cell` stage re-optimises the scale jointly and a mediocre stage-1
   scale costs iterations, never the answer; and past the ~1 % precondition
   the frozen evaluation windows mean **no** order walks the cell in — the
   package's answer to a far cell is `MODEL_FAR_FROM_DATA` and re-indexing
   (§1, §7d), not a different recipe. No preset changed (this WP's non-goal;
   nothing measured here says one should be).
4. **Weighted difference plots — already the invariant, adopt by citation.**
   Toby gives three reasons the Δ/σ difference beats the raw one. Every
   difference curve this package draws has been Δ/σ since WP-1029
   (`result.sig()`, one authority); §5 now cites the paper for it.
5. **"Not all of these parameters are appropriate to vary" — already
   structural.** Toby's own caveat on his mechanism (his Fig. 2c: an
   instrumental broadening term out-derivatives the sample term that should
   move). WP-1050's handover records the identical example — the leverage
   ranking *will* rank U/V/W while Layer 2 structurally cannot suggest it —
   and the strategy veto, `mode_fixed` and `lab_sample_refine`'s frozen
   instrument are where the protocol encodes the caveat.

**Open, recorded:** whether a dynamic worst-fit *loop* (suggest → free → fit
→ repeat) beats the presets on the planted-cause fixtures is a measurable
question no session has measured. The eval harness (WP-1052/1053, next round
1107) is where the presets would be seen losing, if they lose. Not a 1107
task; a note for whoever opens that question.

Corroborations with no change needed: the staged-release rationale (§2 —
"parameters may refine to unrealistic values. At this point it becomes
unlikely that including additional parameters … will allow the model to
recover", now cited); scale+background first explained as the
largest-derivative groups rather than tradition; and the design premise
itself — the paper closes by envisioning software that judges
parameterisation, plausibility and model expansion, which is the FitReport's
job description.
